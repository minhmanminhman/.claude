# Cheatsheet — Decision Rules & Revision Techniques

## The Master Rule
**Break a rule only when you can name the compensating merit.** If you cannot name it, follow the rule. (Ch 1)

## Revision Pass, In Order
| # | Do this | Because |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Destroy *the fact that*, *the reason why is that*, *as to whether*, *in terms of*, *one of the most* | Rule 17 — each has a shorter form, always |
| 2 | Delete *rather, very, little, pretty* | Reminder 8 — they suck the blood of words |
| 3 | Convert *there is / there were / could be heard* to a transitive verb | Rule 14 — stronger, and shorter as a by-product |
| 4 | Rewrite every *not X* as the word meaning *not X* | Rule 15 — the reader wants what *is* |
| 5 | Replace each abstraction with the thing that happened | Rule 16 — particulars hold attention |
| 6 | Move the new information to the end of its sentence | Rule 22 — emphasis is positional |
| 7 | Align ideas of like function into like form | Rule 19 — structure is read before meaning |
| 8 | Read dialogue aloud; reposition attributives at the breath | Reminder 13 |

## Technique Notes (the non-obvious half)
- **Combine the step-by-step series** — one complex idea spread over several short sentences → write it as one, subordinating the supporting facts. Stop when the sentence needs a second reading. (Rule 17)
- **Keep the passive** when the need to make a particular word the *subject* outweighs vigor. Voice follows the sentence's topic, not a blanket rule. (Rule 14)
- **Keep *not*** for genuine denial and for antithesis, where it is strongest: *Not charity, but simple justice.* (Rule 15)
- **Parallel-form alignment** — repeat the article or preposition before every term or only before the first, never some. For twenty parallel ideas, group them and apply the principle within each group, or use a table. (Rule 19)
- **Emphatic-end reordering** — logical predicate last, given information (cause, condition, time) first. To emphasize a *subject*, move it to the predicate's slot: *Through the middle of the valley flowed a winding stream.* (Rule 22)
- **Start fresh on a mired sentence** — rewritten three times and still resisting? Abandon it; write the thought again as two shorter sentences. You lose the good phrasing, but the construction "has become too involved at some point" and patching will not reach it. (Reminder 16)

## Punctuation Decision Tree
- Two complete clauses, no conjunction → **semicolon** (period is correct but weaker). Very short, parallel, conversational → comma is fine (*Man proposes, God disposes*).
- Two complete clauses, with conjunction → **comma before the conjunction**.
- Second clause led by *accordingly, besides, then, therefore, thus* → **semicolon** (these are not conjunctions).
- Clause identifies *which one* → **no commas**, use *that*. Merely adds a fact → **commas both sides**, use *which*.
- List, appositive, amplification, or quotation after a complete clause → **colon**. Never after a verb or preposition.
- Abrupt break, or a common mark is inadequate → **dash**. One dashed interruption per sentence, not two.

## Which Word
| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Distance, or time/quantity? | *farther* / *further* |
| Number, or quantity? | *fewer* / *less* |
| Two, or more than two collectively? | *between* / *among* |
| Impartial, or bored? | *disinterested* / *uninterested* |
| Speaker, or hearer? | *implies* / *infers* |
| Spoken, or word-for-word? | *oral* / *verbal* |
| Governs a noun, or a clause? | *like* / *as* |
| Can it be *use*? | then not *utilize* |
| Ends in *-ize* and feels new? | don't |

## Thresholds & Defaults
- **Paragraph** = one topic. Single-sentence paragraphs only for transitions, or one per speaker in dialogue.
- **Split infinitive**: avoid — unless the adverb takes unusual stress and the ear prefers it.
- **Terminal preposition**: allowed, sometimes better. *"the tool he murdered her with"* beats *"the tool with which he murdered her"* — it sounds more like murder.
- **Passive voice**: allowed when the need to make a particular word the subject outweighs directness.
- **Exclamation mark**: true exclamations and commands only.
- **Numbers**: figures everywhere; spelled out in dialogue.
- **Initialisms**: full name first use, abbreviation after.

## Tells & Smells
| If you see… | You are probably… |
|---|---|
| A run of two-clause sentences joined by *and*/*but*/*while* | producing singsong — recast some (Rule 18) |
| *It is interesting that…* / *A funny thing happened…* | announcing an effect instead of creating one |
| *facility, thrust, meaningful, insightful, -oriented* | inflating the commonplace |
| Every sentence carrying *would, may, might, could* | draining your own authority (Rule 15) |
| A clause between subject and its verb | interrupting the main clause — move it to the front (Rule 20) |
| Adjectives propping up your nouns | working with the wrong nouns (Reminder 4) |
| *This* referring to a whole previous sentence | leaving the referent unnamed (Ch 5) |
| Slang in quotation marks | putting on airs (Ch 4) |
| A superlative you enjoyed writing | overstating, and forfeiting the reader's trust (Reminder 7) |

## When the Rules Run Out
Correctness is the floor, not the ceiling. Paine's *"These are the times that try men's souls"* has grammatically correct variants that are all "marked for oblivion." Past this point it is ear — and ear comes from reading well, not from rules.
**Err conservative**: "there is simply a better chance of doing well if the writer holds a steady course, enters the stream of English quietly, and does not thrash about."
