# Chapter 4: III. A Few Matters of Form

## Core Idea
Mechanical conventions, alphabetized. No argument, no theory — settled practice for the surface details that otherwise cost the reader attention.

## Key Concepts
- **Colloquialisms** — Use one plainly or not at all. Never in quotation marks: "to do so is to put on airs, as though you were inviting the reader to join you in a select society of those who know better."
- **Exclamations** — Do not emphasize a simple statement with `!`. Reserve it for true exclamations and commands: *What a wonderful show!* *Halt!*
- **Headings** — Leave space at the top of page 1 for the editor. Omit the period after a title or heading; `?` and `!` are allowed if the heading calls for them.
- **Hyphen** — Required for most compound adjectives (*leisure-class pursuits*, *round-the-island race*); not between words better written as one (*waterfowl*). The language trends toward union: *bed chamber → bed-chamber → bedchamber*. A dictionary beats instinct.
  - Cautionary tale: *The Chattanooga News-Free Press* — "we ask too much of a hyphen when we ask it to cast its spell over words it does not adjoin."
- **Margins** — Roughly equal left and right; widen the left if heavy annotation is expected.
- **Numerals** — Figures or Roman notation, not spelled out: *August 9, 1988*, *Part XII*, *Rule 3*, *352d Infantry*. Exception: in dialogue, spell them out — *"I arrived home on August ninth."*
- **Parentheses** — Punctuate the enclosing sentence exactly as if the parenthesis were absent; the enclosed expression is punctuated as if it stood alone, minus the final stop unless it is `?` or `!`. (A wholly detached parenthesized sentence takes its stop *inside*.)
- **Quotations** — Formal documentary quotation: colon plus quotation marks. Quotation in apposition or as direct object: comma plus marks. Commas and periods go *inside* the marks by typographical convention "though logically it often seems not to belong there." Block quotations of a full line or more: fresh line, indented, no marks unless in the original. Quotations introduced by *that* are indirect discourse, so no marks: *Keats declares that beauty is truth.* Proverbs and familiar literary phrases need none.
- **References** — Abbreviate frequently cited titles; put references in parentheses or footnotes, not the body. Omit *act, scene, line, book, volume, page* except when referring to only one: *(IV.ii.14)*, *Othello II.iii.264-267*.
- **Syllabication** — Consult a dictionary for end-of-line division.
- **Titles** — Italics with capitalized initials. Omit initial *A*/*The* when a possessive precedes: *Dickens's Tale of Two Cities*.
