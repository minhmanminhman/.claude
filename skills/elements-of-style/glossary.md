# Glossary

Terms the book uses with a specific meaning; each is expanded in the chapter cited.

**Attributive** — The *he said* / *she added* tag in dialogue; place it where a speaker would pause. (Ch 6)

**Compensating merit** — Strunk's escape clause: break a rule only when the sentence gains something nameable in return. (Ch 1)

**Correlative expressions** — Paired connectives (*both/and*, *not only/but also*, *either/or*) requiring the same construction on each side. Rule 19. (Ch 3)

**Ear** — The trained judgment that overrules the handbook on split infinitives, terminal prepositions, and *that*-omission. Reminder 14. (Ch 6)

**Gerund** — A verbal noun; takes the possessive (*Mother objected to **our** driving*), unlike the participle, which takes the objective (*They heard **him** singing*). (Ch 2)

**Loose sentence** — Two clauses, the second on a conjunction or relative. One relieves formality; a run of them is singsong. Rule 18. (Ch 3)

**Logical predicate** — The new element in a sentence; it belongs in the emphatic final position. Rule 22. (Ch 3)

**Nondetachable** — White's term for style: not a sauce added to prose but an expression of self. (Ch 6)

**Parallel construction** — Likeness of form marking likeness of content and function. Rule 19. (Ch 3)

**Parenthetic expression** — Material interrupting a sentence's flow; enclose between commas, both or neither. Rule 3. (Ch 2)

**Participial phrase (dangling)** — An opening participial that fails to refer to the grammatical subject. Rule 11. (Ch 2)

**Periodic sentence** — One withholding its main statement until the end, gaining prominence for it. Rule 22. (Ch 3)

**Positive form** — Stating what *is* rather than what is *not*; *not* reserved for denial and antithesis. Rule 15. (Ch 3)

**Qualifiers** — *rather, very, little, pretty*: "the leeches that infest the pond of prose." Reminder 8. (Ch 6)

**Restrictive / nonrestrictive clause** — Restrictive identifies its antecedent (no commas, *that*); nonrestrictive merely adds to it (commas both sides, *which*). Rule 3. (Ch 2, Ch 5)

**Serial comma** — The comma before the final conjunction in a list of three or more. Rule 2. (Ch 2)

**Style** — "What is distinguished and distinguishing"; the sound the words make on paper. (Ch 6)

**Topic sentence** — The opening sentence of a paragraph, signalling its topic or its transition. Rule 13. (Ch 3)

**Which-hunting** — Deliberately removing defining *whiches* and replacing them with *that*. (Ch 5)
